  1. How a scam works · 01

    It lands like any other text.

    No warning sign, no strange sender. Just a delivery update, the kind most of us get every week.

  2. 02 · The hook

    A deadline, and a tiny fee.

    “Within 24 hours” is there to stop you checking. £1.45 feels too small to question. Real organisations give you time.

  3. 03 · The disguise

    The link isn't who it says.

    Find the first single slash in a web address and read the name just before it. That's who really owns the page. Everything to its left can say anything.

  4. 04 · The trap

    The fee is bait. Your card is the prize.

    The page asks for your full card details. Once typed in, they go straight to the scammer, and the follow-up call “from your bank” often comes next.

    Report it (UK)

    • A text message: forward it to 7726. It's free.
    • An email: forward it to report@phishing.gov.uk.
    • Lost money or shared bank details: call your bank on 159. Then report it to Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland) or to Police Scotland on 101.
